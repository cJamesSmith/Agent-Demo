# Briefing outline

Two pages. Source filename in parentheses after each conclusion.

## 1. The question (2–3 lines)

The question this meeting must answer, and the decision that follows from it.

## 2. Where the problem actually is (half a page)

The two or three places the evidence points. Lead with the figure, then the first-hand account that agrees with it. Distinguish the measured step from the whole process the resident experiences — these are often confused, and the confusion is usually the finding.

## 3. Evidence (half a page)

The figures that carry the argument, each with its file, its period, and its definition. If a definition is easy to misread, state it in one line.

Where two sources disagree, both appear here with both filenames.

## 4. Claims to withdraw (short list)

Public claims the evidence does not support. For each: the claim, where it appears, the file that contradicts it. These are the most urgent items in the brief.

## 5. Decide now

What can be decided at this meeting with the evidence in hand. Each item is specific enough to act on: define the clock, stop a sentence being published, approve a wording.

## 6. Must wait

What cannot be decided yet, and the specific input each item is waiting for. "More analysis" is not an input. A named table arriving on a named date is.

# Six-slide version

1. The question
2. Where the time goes
3. The evidence
4. Claims to delete
5. Decide tomorrow
6. Must wait

One idea per slide. Every figure carries its source filename. No slide asserts something the brief does not support.
