# Competitive Landscape Brief

> Fictional instructional material, updated June 2026.

## Vietnam

- Two local automation vendors are growing rapidly, but their products focus on customer service and marketing.
- International collaboration software primarily serves large multinational enterprises.
- Channel interviews suggest that the market still requires customer education.

**Opportunity**: A gap exists for “rapid deployment + local-language templates” aimed at midsize companies.

**Primary counterargument**: Market education and after-sales investment could push actual customer acquisition cost above the $18 estimate in the research table.

## Indonesia

- Indonesia has the greatest number of local SaaS vendors, with wide variation in features and pricing.
- Two leading platforms already offer basic AI assistants.
- Geographic dispersion across islands makes offline channel coverage expensive.

**Opportunity**: It is the largest market, and vertical-industry solutions still have room to grow.

**Primary counterargument**: More integrations and channel partners are needed, so execution complexity may be underestimated.

## Thailand

- Both international vendors and local implementation providers are relatively mature.
- Most competing products are expensive but have established enterprise customers.
- Three prospective channel partners expressed interest in a joint pilot.

**Opportunity**: Partner-led entry could make cash flow and delivery cadence more predictable.

**Primary counterargument**: Growth is slower, and replacing established vendors requires clear differentiation.

## Conflicting Evidence

The market data table assigns Vietnam a competition score of 58, lower than Thailand's 61. Customer interviews, however, show that Thai customers are more familiar with existing tools. These findings are not necessarily contradictory: the former measures vendor competition, while the latter reflects category maturity. The dashboard must not merge them into a single unexplained fact.
