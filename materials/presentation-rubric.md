# Final presentation — what is required

Day 4, 16:00–17:00. Each team presents for 8 minutes with 2 minutes to change over. If the instructor announces more than six teams, presentations start at 15:45 and are capped at 5 minutes.

Open your actual deliverable on screen. A presentation about a document you do not show does not count.

## The five required elements

**1. The decision or the product, first.**
One sentence, in the first thirty seconds. What did you produce, and what does it tell the reader to do? Do not build up to it.

**2. The source file behind each substantive claim.**
Every figure, deadline, document requirement, and statement that something is available. Name the file. If you cannot name it, do not say it.

**3. One place a person corrected the agent.**
What it produced, what was wrong, how you found it, and what you changed. Be specific — the sentence, not the topic.

A team reporting no corrections either did not check or is not saying. The instructor will ask.

**4. One item left at "to be confirmed".**
Which one, why the pack could not answer it, and who would have to. Leaving a gap visible is the skill; filling it with something plausible is the failure.

**5. Where you used each of the three days' techniques.**
Aimed tool-calls, retrieval with a citation trail, and a planned workflow with delegation and review. One place each. And one place where a technique was the wrong tool and you stopped using it.

## Also say, if it applies to your pack

Every Day 4 pack contains at least one contradiction between two files. Say what you found, which source you followed, and why. This is usually the most interesting thing a team has to report.

## What the instructor will ask

Expect at least one of:

- "Where did that number come from?" — pointing at any figure on your screen.
- "What did the agent get wrong that you nearly missed?"
- "Which of your claims would you not be willing to publish?"
- "What did you decide not to do, and why?"
- "Your two documents both give a day count. Do they match?"

## How this is scored

| Dimension | Weight | What is looked for |
|---|---:|---|
| **Problem definition and public-service value** | 15% | The task and the resident it serves are clear, and the work answers a real need rather than a restated brief. |
| **Completeness of the deliverables** | 25% | All three deliverables exist, are usable end to end, and can be shown on screen. |
| **Appropriate use of the week's techniques** | 25% | Tool-calls aimed at the right files, retrieval with a citation trail, and a planned workflow with a review step — used where each was warranted, not everywhere. |
| **Accuracy, robustness, and error handling** | 15% | Test cases were run and reported. Gaps read "to be confirmed". Contradictions are surfaced with both filenames. |
| **Usability and clarity of the demonstration** | 10% | A real reader could act on the document without telephoning to ask what it means, and the presentation shows it. |
| **Privacy, safety, and social impact** | 10% | Personal details are not carried beyond what the task needs, claims needing a person's approval are marked, and the team can say who is worst affected if the work is wrong. |

Underneath all six, five things the instructor is checking for:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Traceable** | Every substantive claim has a named source file. |
| **Honest** | Gaps are marked, not filled. Contradictions are surfaced, not smoothed. |
| **Consistent** | Your own deliverables do not contradict each other. |
| **Usable** | A real reader could act on it without telephoning to ask what it means. |
| **Owned** | The team can defend any sentence in it without re-reading the pack. |

A plain document that meets all five beats a polished one that does not. The two dimensions teams most often lose are accuracy and error handling — because the test cases were never run — and privacy, safety, and social impact, because "we did not include personal data" is not an answer to it. One honest paragraph scores that dimension; a disclaimer does not.

## What fails

- A figure that is not in the pack.
- A claim whose meaning has drifted from its source — particularly a short attractive number that belongs to one step being presented as covering the whole process.
- Vagueness where a specific reason was required.
- Two of your own documents giving different day counts.
- "The agent produced it" as the answer to where something came from.

You are signing this work. Present it that way.
