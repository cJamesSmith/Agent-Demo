# Family allowance desk — intake notes

Working notes kept by desk officers and the desk supervisor, Public Service Centre No. 3, Samarkand. Not a rule document. Invented for a training exercise.

---

**Desk A, 28 October 2026.** Eleven residents at the allowance desk today, four turned away without an application being recorded. Three of the four were missing the income confirmation for one of the three months. The fourth had a household composition certificate more than six months old; we accepted it because nothing says it expires, but the officer was not sure.

---

**Desk A, 30 October 2026.** Standing practice at this desk, written down because two officers asked the same question this week: we require a resident to show that the household has been registered at a Samarkand region address for **at least twelve months** before we record an allowance application. Residents registered here more recently are told to apply next year.

Where this came from: it was the practice at this desk before the 1 September rules were issued, and it was in the 4 March version. Nobody has told us to stop. The supervisor has asked the regional office twice — 12 October and 26 October — and has had no written reply.

Two officers have pointed out that the 1 September document does not contain this condition and lists only registered residence in the Republic of Uzbekistan. The desk is still applying the twelve months.

This affects mostly households arriving from other regions, and this year that has mostly meant households from the Republic of Karakalpakstan.

---

**Desk B, 2 November 2026.** The income question residents find hardest is not the threshold, it is the three months. A resident whose income changed in the middle of the three months, or who changed employer, cannot produce a single certificate that covers the period, and does not understand why we are asking about August when they are applying in November.

Six residents in the last two weeks have come back a second time solely because of a missing month.

---

**Desk B, 3 November 2026.** Asked the supervisor about one-off payments. A resident had a redundancy payment in one month and it put the three-month average above the threshold. Supervisor's view: the average is the average, and the desk has no authority to exclude a payment. The commission decides, so the desk records the application and includes the employer's note explaining the payment.

Recording the application is the officer's duty in any case. We record it and let the commission decide. We do not refuse at the desk on income.

---

**Desk A, 4 November 2026.** Residents keep asking two things we cannot answer: how much the allowance is, and how long the decision takes. We have nothing in writing for either. Officers are giving residents their own estimates for the waiting time, and the estimates differ between desks — one officer said two weeks, another said a month. Both were guessing.

The supervisor has asked that officers stop giving a figure.

---

**Desk B, 5 November 2026.** A resident asked how to appeal a refusal. We do not have an appeal route in writing. The officer told her to come back and ask for the supervisor, which is not an appeal.

---

**Supervisor, 6 November 2026.** Pension statements are a recurring problem where the pensioner is not the applicant. The rules require income confirmation for each earning member, and a pension is income, but the pensioner is often not the person at the desk and does not attend. We have no instruction on whether the applicant may obtain a household member's pension statement, or whether the pensioner must come in person.

Current practice varies by officer, which is the worst of the available answers.

---

**Supervisor, 7 November 2026.** For the director's attention. The twelve-month registration question needs settling in writing this month. We are turning residents away on a condition that is not in the current rules, and at least one resident has now asked us which document requires it. We could not show her one.
