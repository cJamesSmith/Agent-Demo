# Counting and weighting rules

Training exercise. Fictional internal rules for handling a consultation.

## Before you count anything

Apply these three rules in order and report how many rows each one removed or set aside. A count published without this step is not defensible.

### 1. Duplicates

A row with a value in `duplicate_of` is an exact resubmission of the response named in that column. The portal did not block double submissions during the consultation.

Remove duplicates from every count. Report how many you removed.

### 2. Campaign responses

A row with a value in `campaign_id` came through an organised campaign using identical wording. Rows sharing a `campaign_id` are one coordinated position.

These are real people and they are not discarded. But an organised campaign is not the same evidence as the same number of people arriving at the same view independently, and a table that merges the two misleads the reader.

Count campaign responses **separately**. Report both figures every time: the individual count, and the campaign count beside it. Never publish a combined total without also publishing the split.

Where a campaign respondent wrote individual text on a different article, that comment counts as an individual response **on that article only**.

### 3. No comment

`no_comment` means the respondent did not address that article. It is not support and it is not opposition.

Never treat silence as agreement. Percentages are calculated on those who commented on that article, and the base must be stated every time. "62 percent supported Article 3" is not a usable sentence. "Of the 209 individual respondents who commented on Article 3, 171 supported it" is.

## Weighing opinion once it is counted

A count tells you how many. It does not tell you how much a view should weigh.

**Who is affected.** A respondent who will live with the consequence of an article carries more weight on that article than one who will not. Where opposition to an article is concentrated in one region, report the concentration, not only the national total. A view held by a small number of people who are the only ones affected is not a minority view about them.

**Whether the respondent knows the thing they are describing.** Centre staff and regional government describe operations directly. Residents describe their own experience directly. Neither is authoritative about the other.

**Whether the argument is testable.** An argument that names something checkable is stronger than one that states a preference. Note which arguments the Agency could verify.

## What the numbers cannot tell you

This consultation heard from people who knew it was running, could read the draft, and could respond in Uzbek or Russian through the portal. It did not hear from the people least able to reach the service.

Do not write that the public supports something. Write that a stated number of respondents, out of a stated base, supported it.

## Required in every table

- The base for each percentage.
- Individual and campaign figures shown separately.
- Duplicates removed and the number stated.
- The rule you applied where a response was ambiguous.
