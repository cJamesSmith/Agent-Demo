# Day 4 task — public consultation synthesis

Training exercise. Use only the files in this folder.

The Agency ran a four-week public consultation on the draft decision in `draft-articles.md`. It closed on 7 November with 412 responses. You are the team that has to tell the deputy what the public actually said, before the draft is signed.

Produce:

1. `synthesis.md` — what the responses say, article by article. For each article: the weight of opinion, the strongest argument on each side, and whether the responses raise anything the draft does not address at all.
2. `decision-note.md` — one page for the deputy. For each of the four articles: keep, amend, or withdraw, with the reason and the evidence. Where you recommend an amendment, give the wording.
3. `response-table.md` — the counts, as you counted them, with your counting rules stated. A reader must be able to reproduce your numbers.

Answer in `synthesis.md`:

1. Which article attracted the most opposition, and is that opposition broad or concentrated?
2. Where does a numerical majority point one way and the better argument the other? Say which you followed.
3. What did respondents raise that the draft does not cover?

Rules:

- Every count must be reproducible from `responses.csv` under the rules you state. A number a reader cannot reproduce is not evidence.
- Apply `weighting-rules.md` to duplicates and campaign responses before counting anything. State how many responses each rule removed or set aside.
- A campaign response is still a response from a real person. Do not discard it; count it separately and say why.
- Do not report a percentage without the base it is a percentage of.
- Do not treat silence as agreement. Most respondents did not comment on most articles.
- If the pack does not state something, write "to be confirmed".

Done: every article in `draft-articles.md` has a recommendation with evidence, every number in your documents can be reproduced from `responses.csv`, and your counting rules are written down before your counts.
