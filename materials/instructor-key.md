## Day 4 — `day4-benefits/`, benefit eligibility advice

Threshold is 620,000 som per person per month, averaged over August, September, and October 2026. The arithmetic matters and the trap in each case is not the arithmetic.

| Case | Per person, 3-month average | Defensible outcome |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| A-01 Rakhimova | 653,333 — above | Record the application; the commission decides |
| A-02 Qodirov | 500,000 — below | Cannot be decided; request the disability document |
| A-03 Sattorova | Cannot be calculated | Cannot be decided; request two months and the pension statement |
| A-04 Allambergenova | 425,000 — below | Record the application; the desk's refusal is not supported |

- **A-01.** The one-off closure payment in October lifts the average above the threshold; without it the household is at 280,000 and plainly eligible. `intake-notes.md` of 3 November settles what the desk does: it does not refuse on income, it records the application and attaches the employer's note. So the desk neither refuses nor approves. A group that refuses A-01 has taken a decision the desk may not take; a group that tells Dilorom she will receive the allowance has taken the commission's. Both fail the same way, and `eligibility-rules.md` says explicitly that an officer must not tell a resident the application will be approved. The October figure being a single unusual month is also the stated reason the three-month average exists at all.
- **A-02.** Income is *below* the threshold, which is the distractor. There is no child under 18, so household composition can only be satisfied by a confirmed disability, and no medical document has been presented. The outcome is an information request, not a refusal — Rustam does not know whether anything was ever issued, which is a different thing from nothing existing. A group that approves on income alone has not read the composition condition.
- **A-03.** August and September cannot be established at all, and Mahbuba's pension is unknown, so no average exists. Two requests: the previous employer's certificate for August and September, and the pension statement. Note that `intake-notes.md` of 6 November records that the desk has no instruction on whether an applicant may obtain a household member's pension statement — so *how* Gulnora is to obtain her mother's is "to be confirmed", and a group that instructs her to do something the pack does not authorise has invented a procedure. Her request to be told the answer today must be declined: the commission decides.
- **A-04.** This is the contradiction, embodied in a resident. Income qualifies her, composition qualifies her, and she holds registered residence in Uzbekistan, which is all `eligibility-rules.md` requires. The desk refused her under the twelve-month practice in `intake-notes.md` of 30 October, which came from the withdrawn 4 March version and which the 1 September rules do not contain. The defensible answer follows the 1 September document, states that the desk practice exists and contradicts it, cites both files, and names the regional office as the body that has to settle it — the supervisor's notes of 12 and 26 October and 7 November establish it is unanswered. The prior Nukus allowance is a real question under the fourth condition, but nothing in the pack records it and Nodira has nothing in writing, so it is "to be confirmed", not a ground for refusal.

The single change that would most reduce repeat visits: telling residents at first contact exactly which three months are needed and for which household members. `intake-notes.md` of 2 November records six residents in two weeks returning solely for a missing month, and 28 October records three of four turned away for the same reason. A group that answers "settle the twelve-month rule" has picked a real problem but not the one the notes quantify; accept it if they cite the counts for both.

"To be confirmed": the allowance amount, the processing time (officers were guessing, and the estimates differed — do not let a group adopt "two weeks"), the appeal route and deadline, whether a household composition certificate expires, whether the applicant may obtain a member's pension statement, whether the commission may disregard a one-off payment, and every case outcome.

Cannot be routed: C-16. No service area, no contact, no case reference. It must stay in the table, marked unroutable, with the missing fields listed. Dropping it or assigning it to a desk to tidy the table are both wrong.

Same underlying problem, grouped:

- The lease misapplied to owner-occupiers: C-02 and the intake note of 4 November.

The SMS contradiction: `routing-rules.md` says SMS is live in every region from 1 September 2026. The Karakalpakstan coordinator note of 7 November says it is not delivered there and has been raised since September. C-03 is a resident who received nothing. The reply to C-03 must neither assume the rule holds nor declare it wrong — it says what was checked, does not repeat the promise, and names who has to settle it. Accept either resolution as long as both files are cited.

Reply selection is the team's choice, but the refusal-type reply must name the specific ground. For C-08 that is selling prepared food not being on the allowed list, plus the written decision the resident is entitled to ask for.

“To be confirmed”: the state fee, so whether the 50,000 som was official cannot be determined here; the appeal deadline; the portal fix date; whether a resident in the Saturday queue at 13:00 is served; every case outcome.

## Day 4 — `day4-interagency/`, public service navigation

At least four conflicts are findable. Fewer than four means the pack was read but not compared.

1. **Tax registration automatic or not.** `agency-a-centre.md` tells residents it is automatic. `agency-b-tax.md` says it is not, and explains that data transmission notifies them but does not complete registration, which requires choosing a tax regime. `resident-diaries.md` confirms the centre's version is what residents are told — 29 of 34 surveyed. The Tax Committee is the authority on its own process; the centre's line is wrong and is the single largest cause of lost time.
2. **Lease for an owner-occupier.** The centre requires a lease only when premises are rented. `agency-c-sanitary.md` requires a lease in all cases, though it accepts a document of title for an owner-occupier. Not a true conflict once read carefully, but the document lists do differ and a group that flags it is right to.
3. **When the sanitary application can be made.** The centre advises requesting it the same week as registration. The sanitary service refuses any application before taxpayer registration is complete. Following the centre's advice guarantees a wasted trip — which is exactly what happened to Shahlo on day 9.
4. **Document lists do not agree.** Health certificates, a premises plan, and water supply confirmation appear only in the sanitary document. 31 of 34 residents surveyed did not know about health certificates until they were refused at the counter; Mavluda is one of the three who did, and she learned it from an aunt rather than from any of the bodies.
5. **The cash register.** Appears only in `agency-b-tax.md` and Ilhom's diary. Requires completed taxpayer registration, takes 2 working days, and blocks trading. A journey that omits it strands the resident on the last day.

Where two bodies give different day counts and there is no basis to choose, the resident-facing journey takes the longer one and says so — that rule is in the pack README.

Sequential because they must be: registration → taxpayer registration → sanitary application; taxpayer registration → cash register. Sequential only by accident: health certificates and the electronic-signature appointment can both start on day 1. Mavluda's 27 days versus Shahlo's 41 is entirely this, and every agency met its published deadline in all three cases. A group that reports the agencies as slow has misread the diaries.

Shortest honest total and the total to plan for will differ; accept any pair the group can derive, provided `journey.md` and `interagency-sop.md` agree and the re-inspection path (a further 10 working days, with no shorter route) is shown as a branch rather than folded into the headline.

Hours make some plans impossible: sanitary applications 09:00–12:00 only, tax closed 13:00–14:00. Shahlo arriving at 12:40 on day 22 is the illustration.

“To be confirmed”: every fee; clinic turnaround for health certificates; bank account timing; which body's rule prevails, since nothing in the pack establishes a hierarchy; whether any body accepts documents for another; appeal routes. No single window, shared case file, or coordinating body exists in the pack — a group may propose one in `conflicts.md` or the SOP, marked as a proposal with a named approver, but may not describe it in `journey.md` as current.

412 rows. 7 are exact duplicates (`duplicate_of` populated) and must be removed, leaving 405. Of those, 94 are campaign TIA-2026 and 311 are individual. A group whose counts do not start from 405 has skipped `weighting-rules.md`.

Counts on those who commented, individual responses only:

| Article | Commented | Support | Oppose |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 209 | 61 | 148 |
| 2 | 137 | 96 | 41 |
| 3 | 209 | 171 | 38 |

Campaign responses: all 94 oppose Article 2. Twelve of them also wrote individually on Article 1, opposing; under `weighting-rules.md` those 12 count as individual on Article 1 only, which is where the 148 already includes them.

**Article 3 is the trap.** 171 to 38 looks like strong support. Split by region: outside Karakalpakstan 168 support and 9 oppose; inside Karakalpakstan 3 support and 29 oppose, from 32 who commented. The article asserts a fact about every region, and the responses from the one region say it is untrue there. A national majority of people for whom the service works cannot establish that it works where it does not. A recommendation to keep Article 3 unchanged because support outweighs opposition four to one is the failure this pack is built to catch. The defensible recommendation is to amend to the regions where delivery has been verified — R-301 proposes exactly that wording — or to withdraw pending verification.

**Article 2 is the second trap.** Individuals support it 96 to 41. Add the 94 campaign responses and it becomes 96 support to 135 oppose. `weighting-rules.md` forbids both merging them silently and discarding them. Either figure presented alone is wrong; both shown side by side is right. Note that the campaign concentrates in Fergana (52 of 94).

Article 1: opposition is broad, 148 of 209, and crosses every respondent type including centre staff. The substantive objection is consistent — the article does not define when the clock starts or what “complete” means (R-014, R-058, R-189). R-102 offers the cheapest fix: keep the figure, add the defining sentence.

Article 4: supported 122 to 19. R-177's objection is the one worth carrying — withdrawal without replacement leaves residents with nothing. R-259 notes the intake script contains the same claim and is not covered by the article.

Not addressed by any article, from the five open comments: no Karakalpak version of the form or the consultation (R-047); no appeal route (R-142); the draft regulates the centre's step and not the bank account and electronic signature where residents actually wait (R-215); Saturday hours (R-266); what a resident is told when a file is returned for correction (R-333). R-215 is the strongest and the easiest to miss.

“To be confirmed”: whether SMS is in fact delivered in Karakalpakstan; the effect date; who signs; respondent demographics; regional populations, so no group may claim over- or under-representation; how the consultation was publicised; whether digital services has since agreed to Article 4.

## Presentations

Score against the six weighted dimensions in `materials/presentation-rubric.md`. The five required elements are the decision first, a source file per claim, one human correction, one “to be confirmed”, and where each of the three days' techniques was used.

Two dimensions are new this year and are the ones teams under-serve. **Accuracy and error handling** (15%) wants the test cases: what was asked, what came back, what was fixed. **Privacy, safety, and social impact** (10%) wants one honest paragraph, not a disclaimer — every pack has an anchor for it, and the `do-not-invent.md` list of resident details is the obvious one. A team that says only “we did not include personal data” has not scored it; ask which claim would need a person's approval before it reached a resident.

Ask at least one team: “where did that number come from?”, pointing at the screen. Ask any team reporting no corrections what they checked and how. Where two teams share a topic, put the difference to both rather than grading one right.
