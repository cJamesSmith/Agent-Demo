# Not in the files — do not fill these in

Each item must read "to be confirmed" wherever your documents need it.

- Any fee in som: registration, cash register, sanitary permit, health certificate.
- How long a district clinic takes to issue a health certificate. One diary reports 6 days; no agency document states a standard.
- How long a bank takes to open an account.
- Which body's rule prevails when two conflict. No file in this pack establishes a hierarchy.
- Whether any of the three bodies can accept a document on another's behalf.
- Whether the sanitary service will accept an application with taxpayer registration still pending.
- Any appeal route or deadline. None of the three documents mentions one.
- The date of the next inter-agency meeting, or who chairs it.

## Do not invent a solution the files do not contain

There is no single window, no shared case file, and no coordinating body in this pack. It is tempting to recommend one, and it may even be right, but you cannot describe it in `journey.md` as though it exists. A proposal belongs in `conflicts.md` or in the internal SOP, marked as a proposal with a named approver.

## The conflicts are the task

At least four places where the three bodies disagree are findable in these files. A `conflicts.md` that lists fewer than four has not been finished. Each conflict must name both documents.

Areas worth checking closely: what the centre tells residents about tax registration against what the Tax Committee says; whether a lease is required for an owner-occupier; whether the sanitary application can be requested in the week after registration; and the document lists, which do not agree on what the resident must produce.

## The finding that is not a conflict

Three residents took 41, 63, and 27 days. Every agency met its own published deadline in every case. The difference between 27 and 41 days is not any agency being slow — it is the order the resident happened to use, and one resident only knew the order because a relative told her.

Your answer to "where does the resident actually wait" should distinguish waiting for a body to act from waiting because nobody said what to do next. They have different fixes, and only one of them costs anything.

## Two smaller traps

The sanitary service accepts applications 09:00–12:00 only, and the Tax Committee closes 13:00–14:00. A journey that says "attend the tax office and then the sanitary service the same afternoon" is impossible on the face of these documents.

The cash register step is invisible until it blocks opening. It appears only in the Tax Committee document and one diary. A journey that omits it will read as complete and will strand the resident on the last day.
