# Tuesday role cards

Print this page and cut along the lines. Hand one card to each group at the start of the Tuesday lab. Do not hand out the whole page — a group that reads all three cards has lost the exercise.

With more than three groups, repeat a role. Two groups with the same role reach different conclusions often enough to be worth the debrief.

The cards do not restrict which files a group may read. Every group has the whole pack. The card decides what the group leads with, and what it is prepared to be asked about at the meeting.

---

## Card 1 — Economy

**Your priority: time to operate.**

The deputy's meeting is about why residents do not experience "15 minutes". You answer for the elapsed time, from a resident's first contact to the point where they can lawfully operate.

Lead with:

1. Where the days actually go, by service area. The registration step and the total are not the same number, and your briefing must not let them be read as the same number.
2. Which part of the elapsed time this agency controls, and which part it does not.
3. What the deputy can decide tomorrow that would move days rather than perception.

Read `october-snapshot.md` for the definition of `avg_days_to_operate` before you quote it. If your briefing uses that column without stating what it measures, a reader will assume it is the 30 minutes.

Be ready to be asked: which single figure in your briefing would most embarrass the deputy if a journalist quoted it back, and is it correct?

---

## Card 2 — Digital

**Your priority: repeated returns and the form.**

You answer for applications that come back for correction. A file returned once is a resident who makes a second trip, and none of that appears in the registration time.

Lead with:

1. Which service area returns files most often. The table gives you one month. Say so, and say what in the pack tells you whether that month is typical.
2. What the pack says is causing it. Two files give different causes, and they were written two days apart. Give both, with both filenames. You may say which you find more credible and why; you may not present one as settled.
3. Which of the existing claims about the form and about status notification the other files support, and which they do not.

Be ready to be asked: if the cause named in the older file was fixed, why is the rate still where it is?

---

## Card 3 — Social

**Your priority: access in Karakalpakstan.**

You answer for the residents furthest from the service. A national average is not a defence to someone for whom the service does not work.

Lead with:

1. How Karakalpakstan compares on time, on staff, and on satisfaction — and what the load per officer is, which no column gives you directly.
2. Whether the improvements described in the existing deck actually reached this region. Check the staffing and the notification claims against the figures and against the first-hand accounts, not against the deck.
3. What the deputy can decide tomorrow, given that the pilot choice is not tomorrow's decision.

`public-comments.md` is evidence. A resident reporting that something promised has not arrived is a first-hand account of the thing the deck asserts.

Be ready to be asked: you have the smallest application volume in the country. Why should the quarter's attention go here?

---

## For the instructor

All three roles hit the same four unsupported claims from a different angle, which is the point of the debrief — the claims are wrong regardless of which priority you hold.

The Economy card leads to the clock definition. The Digital card leads to the two sets of minutes disagreeing. The Social card leads to the gap between what was announced and what arrived. The answers are in `instructor-key.md`, Tuesday section.

If a group's briefing could have been written from any of the three cards, the group did not use its card.
